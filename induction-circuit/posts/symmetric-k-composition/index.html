1 Why positive eigenvalues suggest matching

1.1 The joint matrix of a writer and a reader

In the induction pattern [A][B]…[A]→[B][A][B]\ldots[A]\to[B], two attention heads work together. At the historical BB position, an earlier head—the writer ww—uses its QK circuit to attend to the preceding AA. Its value projection VwV_w reads the residual stream at AA, and its output projection OwO_w writes the resulting information into the residual stream at BB. The later reader applies KrK_r to this updated residual stream to form its key at historical BB, containing information about the preceding AA.

At the current AA position, the reader rr uses QrQ_r to form a query from the current residual stream. Its QK circuit compares this query with the keys at historical positions. For induction, this comparison favors the historical BB position because its key carries information about an AA matching the current token. The reader’s OV circuit then reads information about the historical successor BB from its residual stream and writes it into the residual stream at the current position, supporting the prediction of BB.

Using column vectors, define their K-composition matrix and its bilinear score by Cr,w=Qr⊤KrOwVw,sC(x,y)=x⊤Cr,wy.C_{r,w}=Q_r^\top K_rO_wV_w,\qquad s_C(x,y)=x^\top C_{r,w}y.

(1)

Here x,y∈ℝdx,y\in\mathbb{R}^d are reader and writer inputs: xx represents current AA, while yy can represent any historical source token, matching or competing. The projection dimensions are Qr,Kr∈ℝhr×dQ_r,K_r\in\mathbb{R}^{h_r\times d}, Vw∈ℝhw×dV_w\in\mathbb{R}^{h_w\times d}, and Ow∈ℝd×hwO_w\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times h_w}. The source contributes awKrOwVwya_wK_rO_wV_wy to the reader’s key at a candidate position, with writer attention weight awa_w. A previous-token writer typically gives the preceding token most weight. We omit awa_w and analyze x⊤Cr,wyx^\top C_{r,w}y.

A central question here is how this composition assigns a higher attention score to xx and yy representing the same token identity than to inputs representing different tokens, so that the reader can attend to and read from the correct historical successor.

1.2 A spectral clue, rather than a ranking conclusion

In 2022, Anthropic associated positive eigenvalues of the token-space matrix in a pure K-composition term with “same-matching” [2]. For one reader–writer pair, that matrix is Wr,w=E⊤Cr,wEW_{r,w}=E^\top C_{r,w}E, where EE maps one-hot token vectors to embeddings. Both contain the same QK–OV product, but Wr,wW_{r,w} includes embeddings and acts on one-hot inputs, while Cr,wC_{r,w} acts on residual representations. For the same one-hot vector tt on both sides, t⊤Wr,wt=(Et)⊤Cr,w(Et)t^\top W_{r,w}t=(Et)^\top C_{r,w}(Et).

Anthropic weighted these matrices by pwp_w, the earlier head’s mean attention to the immediately preceding position in example text, and computed the trace of their sum: W̃r=∑w:ℓw<ℓrpwWr,w,tr⁡(W̃r)=∑jλj(W̃r),\widetilde W_r=\sum_{w:\,\ell_w<\ell_r}p_w W_{r,w},\qquad \operatorname{tr}(\widetilde W_r)=\sum_j\lambda_j(\widetilde W_r), where ℓw\ell_w and ℓr\ell_r denote the heads’ layers. Anthropic reported a correlation with behavioral prefix-matching scores in their small models [2].

The distinction between token embeddings and deep residual-stream representations matters here. Anthropic’s statistic selects pure K-composition paths: the query is projected directly from the embedding, while the key-side path passes the embedding through the writer’s OV matrix and then the reader’s K projection [2]. Deep residual inputs, however, contain not just token embeddings but also contributions from earlier attention heads and, when present, MLPs. We therefore use x⊤Cr,wyx^\top C_{r,w}y to study matching between these contextual representations without restricting them to embeddings.

1.3 Selecting the reader–writer pair that defines CC

The transformer architecture does not specify a unique pairing between writer and reader heads. These are separate attention heads connected through the shared residual stream: multiple earlier heads can contribute information available to a later reader, and one earlier head can contribute to multiple readers. For the OLMo matrix surveys, we select one writer for each reader using the parameter-based criterion below. The saved Pythia token experiments use the fixed compositions specified in Section 3.2.1.

For a reader rr, the candidate writers are all heads in strictly earlier layers, irrespective of their previous-token behavioral labels. Let PrP_r be the orthogonal projector onto the row space of the reader’s key matrix KrK_r. For each candidate ww, define capture⁡(r,w)=∥PrOwVw∥F2∥OwVw∥F2.\operatorname{capture}(r,w) =\frac{\left\lVert P_rO_wV_w\right\rVert_F^2}{\left\lVert O_wV_w\right\rVert_F^2}. This measures the fraction of the writer’s OV matrix energy that lies in the residual directions read by KrK_r. We select w*(r)=arg maxw:ℓw<ℓrcapture⁡(r,w),w^*(r)=\underset{w:\,\ell_w<\ell_r}{\operatorname{arg\,max}} \operatorname{capture}(r,w), where ℓw\ell_w and ℓr\ell_r are the heads’ layers. A reader in the first layer has no earlier candidate.

The selected writer defines Cr,w*(r)=Qr⊤KrOw*(r)Vw*(r)C_{r,w^*(r)}=Q_r^\top K_rO_{w^*(r)}V_{w^*(r)}. The selected pair remains fixed throughout the distance analysis, while its RoPE-dependent matrix CDC_D varies with DD.

Attention heads selected by this criterion generally exhibit some degree of previous-token attention, but the criterion does not guarantee the strength of this behavior.

2 Decomposing the matrix and its energy

2.1 Positive, negative, and antisymmetric contributions

Every real square matrix has the exact decomposition H=C+C⊤2,A=C−C⊤2,C=H+A.H=\frac{C+C^\top}{2},\qquad A=\frac{C-C^\top}{2},\qquad C=H+A.

(6)

Because A⊤=−AA^\top=-A, the scalar x⊤Axx^\top Ax is equal to its own negative: x⊤Ax=(x⊤Ax)⊤=x⊤A⊤x=−x⊤Ax=0.x^\top Ax=(x^\top Ax)^\top=x^\top A^\top x=-x^\top Ax=0.

(7)

Consequently x⊤Cx=x⊤Hxx^\top Cx=x^\top Hx. This cancellation holds for the same vector on both sides. It does not remove AA from a cross-context score x⊤Cyx^\top Cy with x≠yx\ne y.

The real symmetric matrix HH has a complete orthonormal eigenbasis. Writing H=UΛU⊤H=U\Lambda U^\top separates mutually orthogonal directions, allowing its quadratic form to be read without nonsymmetric cross terms. Define H+=∑k:λk>0λkukuk⊤,H−=∑k:λk<0λkukuk⊤.{H^+}=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_k>0}\lambda_k u_ku_k^\top,\qquad {H^-}=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_k<0}\lambda_k u_ku_k^\top.

(8)

Here uku_k is the eigenvector for λk\lambda_k, and C=H++H−+AC={H^+}+{H^-}+A. Writing a=U⊤xa=U^\top x gives x⊤H+x=∑k:λk>0λkak2≥0,x⊤H−x=∑k:λk<0λkak2≤0.x^\top{H^+} x=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_k>0}\lambda_k a_k^2\ge0,\qquad x^\top{H^-} x=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_k<0}\lambda_k a_k^2\le0.

(9)

Every squared coordinate ak2a_k^2 is nonnegative, so the retained eigenvalue signs determine both inequalities. Either score is zero exactly when xx has no component in its corresponding positive or negative eigenspace.

The positive component rewards agreement in its weighted directions.

2.2 An additive energy accounting

Define the matrix energy as its squared Frobenius norm. Symmetric and antisymmetric matrices are orthogonal under the Frobenius inner product. In addition, H+{H^+} and H−{H^-} occupy disjoint eigenspaces, so ⟨H+,H−⟩F=0\langle{H^+},{H^-}\rangle_F=0. Expanding the squared norm therefore gives ∥C∥F2=∥H+∥F2+∥H−∥F2+∥A∥F2.\left\lVert C\right\rVert_F^2=\left\lVert {H^+}\right\rVert_F^2+\left\lVert {H^-}\right\rVert_F^2+\left\lVert A\right\rVert_F^2.

(10)

The study cohort contains 700 checkpoints spanning the 14 nominal DataDecide scales. Readers retain their original behavioral labels and selected writers. A reader is included only if its attention-based successor score is strictly above the chosen threshold in each of two existing banks. This score is the average attention probability assigned to the designated correct successor position on repeated token sequences. Appendix A specifies the probe and aggregation.

For each component B∈{H+,H−,A}B\in\{{H^+},{H^-},A\}, we first average ∥BD∥F2\left\lVert B_D\right\rVert_F^2 over every integer distance D=0,…,2047D=0,\ldots,2047, then over eligible readers within a checkpoint, then equally over eligible checkpoints within a scale. 3 Complete matching scores and positive-spectral geometry

We start from the complete matching score to ask when different token identities can each rank their own representations highest. Token representations vary around their respective centers. The full margin connects this requirement to the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of CC, and then identifies the role of eigenvector directions associated with positive eigenvalues of HH within that same comparison.

Let token ii have a center μi\mu_i in a shared space. Two representations xix_i and xi′x_i' can differ through deviations around that same center; a competing token j≠ij\ne i has its own center μj\mu_j: xi=μi+ϵi,xi′=μi+ϵi′,xj=μj+ϵj.x_i=\mu_i+\epsilon_i,\qquad x_i'=\mu_i+\epsilon_i',\qquad x_j=\mu_j+\epsilon_j. The deviations capture variation across contexts and layers. For a matching score s(x,y)s(x,y), define the pairwise margin mij(s):=s(xi,xi′)−s(xi,xj)=s(μi+ϵi,μi+ϵi′)−s(μi+ϵi,μj+ϵj).\begin{aligned} m_{ij}(s)&:=s(x_i,x_i')-s(x_i,x_j)\\ &=s(\mu_i+\epsilon_i,\mu_i+\epsilon_i') -s(\mu_i+\epsilon_i,\mu_j+\epsilon_j). \end{aligned} Self-matching has the strictly highest score when mij(s)>0m_{ij}(s)>0 for every competing representation under the variations being considered. Modern Hopfield networks address a related separation-and-perturbation problem: comparing a perturbed input with fixed stored patterns . In our setting, the representations on both sides of a score vary across contexts and layers, so we expand the deviations of both same-token and different-token representations.

3.1.0.2 Ask for self-matching to win for both token identities.

Keep all deviations in the comparison. Alongside xq=μi+ϵqx_q=\mu_i+\epsilon_q, take a left-input representation xq,j=μj+ϵq,jx_{q,j}=\mu_j+\epsilon_{q,j} of token jj. The H−H^- contribution is nonpositive, while the summed variation can help or hurt. The H+H^+-weighted center gap supplies a nonnegative contribution to this full comparison; its required size depends on the other terms.

Thus bijCb_{ij}^C and the corresponding variation term remain important in each direction, even though the signed center biases cancel in the sum.

One optimization direction for making many different tokens prefer their own representations is therefore to enlarge their H+H^+-weighted center gaps, (μi−μj)⊤H+(μi−μj)(\mu_i-\mu_j)^\top H^+(\mu_i-\mu_j), across token pairs. These gaps supply the positive separation contribution to the summed self-matching margins.

3.1.0.3 Use the observed energy composition to focus the analysis.

The expansion identifies how the positive part contributes to simultaneous matching of the observed representations. The archived OLMo/DataDecide measurements provide a second reason to examine it: H+H^+ is the largest mean Frobenius-energy component at every populated scale in Figure 1 and Table A2.

This concentration may echo the optimization direction above: as training progresses, the model may concentrate positive spectral energy on selected directions that increase Gij+G_{ij}^+ by separating token centers. We therefore examine which directions are selected and how their weights contribute to separation. This is a possible interpretation of the learned concentration, while the complete one-way margins retain the contributions of H−H^-, AA, signed bias, and contextual variation.

3.1.0.4 Resolve the positive contribution in the original space.

Applying the same expansion to the H+H^+ contribution gives mij+=xq⊤H+xi−xq⊤H+xj=(μi+ϵq)⊤H+(μi+ϵi)−(μi+ϵq)⊤H+(μj+ϵj)=μi⊤H+(μi−μj)+ϵq⊤H+(μi−μj)+μi⊤H+(ϵi−ϵj)+ϵq⊤H+(ϵi−ϵj).\begin{split} m_{ij}^+ &=x_q^\top H^+x_i-x_q^\top H^+x_j\\ &=(\mu_i+\epsilon_q)^\top H^+(\mu_i+\epsilon_i) -(\mu_i+\epsilon_q)^\top H^+(\mu_j+\epsilon_j)\\ &=\mu_i^\top H^+(\mu_i-\mu_j) +\epsilon_q^\top H^+(\mu_i-\mu_j)\\ &\quad+\mu_i^\top H^+(\epsilon_i-\epsilon_j) +\epsilon_q^\top H^+(\epsilon_i-\epsilon_j). \end{split}

3.2.1 Representations and behavior-selected composition paths

For each of the 50,277 tokenizer IDs, the capture uses up to sixteen natural occurrences from distinct source documents. Left-context windows end at that token, with lengths L∈{1,8,32,128,512,2048}L\in\{1,8,32,128,512,2048\}. 3.2.3 From sampled attention behavior to matching margins

3.2.5.1 Read the behavior heatmap as a probe measurement.

Figures 2a–2b show attention to the correct historical successor in the independent repeated-sequence scan for the reported paths. Its intensity records behavior on the particular sampled tokens, contexts, and sequence lengths in that scan. It is not the value of mijm_{ij} or a comprehensive ranking of matching ability. The margin summaries above average a broader recorded population of token pairs and natural contexts under the specified operator. Both measurements are informative when their input distributions and comparison weights are kept explicit.

At the trained checkpoint, GG provides the largest positive contribution in the reported model averages to the overall matching margin. The component decomposition explains this contribution in the same score units as the complete comparison, while the probe records how the head behaves on a specified repeated-sequence test.
